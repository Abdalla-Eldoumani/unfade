# Studio notes

## Gotchas
- The studio branch comes from main, so World's xr-check fixes (the take retry, the wait for the fade back) are not on it yet; a rare "takes the print off the line" failure here is that known flake, not the studio.
- Editing a file under src/ while a browser check runs makes Vite reload the page mid-run. Edit between runs.
- `/api/analyze` answers 200 even when Claude fails; the reason arrives as `warning` and the studio shows it in the review step. claude.js makes that reason one plain sentence ending "so place the spots yourself by tapping the photo."
- The depth model holds the page's main thread for the whole run (no animation frames at all for about 5 s here, longer on a phone). Anything that must move meanwhile is a Web Animations opacity or transform animation, which the compositor keeps running. The first frame after the block still carries the time from before it, so read an animated value two frames later (`toward` in `developPrint`).
- Depth Anything's raw edges are already crisp on most photos; its real miss is thin things (a launch tower's lattice gets the sky behind it filled in at tower depth). `snapToPhoto` (guided filter, radius 2, eps 1e-3) opens those up. Radius 4 or more softens clean edges and copies water and fur texture into depth, so compare a cutout (photo kept where depth > 0.5) before changing it. It adds about 0.1 s at 518x350 and 0.3 s at 800x518 on this laptop.
- Playwright screenshots wait for the main thread, so they cannot show what happens during the block; CDP `Page.startScreencast` frames come from the compositor and can.
- Failure without pulling a cable: start the server with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=wrong-key-for-a-failure-test` (the real API answers 401, free) and, for no network, also `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 HTTPS_PROXY=http://127.0.0.1:9` (Node 24 sends fetch through the dead proxy: ECONNREFUSED). Both ended with a memory on the line.

- World Labs (server/worldlabs.js): `is_pano` must be a real boolean (the string "false" is a 422); the image goes inline as `data_base64`, no upload step. marble-1.1 took 319 to 326 s and 1,580 credits ($1.26) for each of five worlds; progress is only a status, never a percentage. Assets: splats 100k (1.4 MB), 150k, 500k (7.9 MB), full_res (30 MB), a 4608x2304 panorama, a collider GLB. The key is only in the server window, so a scratch script that needs it runs there.
- The world box shows only when /api/health says `worlds: true`, is off by default, and the line under it says what happens: about five minutes, the memory works without it. Checked with a CC0 living room: hung at once, "ready" 5 min 26 s later.
- After developing, Develop again rests (disabled) until the title, when or where change, or while the last answer was the fallback: re-developing replaces the spots and any notes on them, so it should never be one stray click away.
- Without a key, `page.route('**/api/analyze', ...)` answering `{ source: 'claude', analysis: {...} }` stands in for Claude, to check the spots arriving, Develop again and the sounds Claude ticks.
- Junk pasted around the key no longer matters: claude.js sends only the key itself, found inside whatever the environment holds (bracketed-paste markers, a carriage return, spaces). A value with no key in it is named in plain words before anything is sent. A 401 or 403 now means the key itself is wrong or cut short. Never put an error's message in the warning, because Node's message for a refused header quotes the whole value. Any shell can rule out the network with a placeholder key: the API answers 401.
- Taps on the photo while it develops (data-state developing or review, before the spots are in) wait in `state.taps` and are added after the analysis's spots, three at most, skipping one that lands on a spot already there. Meanwhile the old dots are cleared and Add a spot rests, and a new photo clears the queue. To land a tap in that second: poll data-state with `polling: 'raf'`, then `page.mouse.click`; a capture-phase click listener records whether the spots were in yet.

## Checking without a person
- One photo onto the line, headless: open `/world.html?debug` (it joins room demo over the server) and `/studio.html`, give the photo to `.print-drop input` (laptop) or `.take-photo input` (phone: 390x844, touch, isMobile), fill the title starting with "test", Develop, tap the photo if no spots came back, Hang it, wait for `unfade.hub.prints` to hold the id, then `DELETE /api/memories/<id>`. With the depth model on this laptop's CPU, develop takes about 15 s and the line about 18 s.
- Phone, emulated (task 3): a 4032x3024 JPEG stored sideways with Orientation 6 and FocalLengthIn35mmFilm 26 (EXIF spliced in after SOI) comes out 1536x2048 with focal35mm 26. With the CPU throttled 4x (`Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`) the line takes 34 s, under the 60 s goal; a real phone's speed still needs the phone. Tap the photo with `touchscreen.tap` only after checking its box is inside the viewport: raw coordinates do not scroll.
- Claude, live (task 1), once ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set where `npm run server` runs: send each test photo through `analyzePhoto` from `/src/studio/analyze.js` in the page (the same 1092 px copy Claude sees), draw the returned x and y on the photo, and read the labels and questions beside the dots. Ten kinds: person, two people, pet, food, room, street, landscape, night, party, selfie (CC0 or public domain from Wikimedia Commons, kept outside the repo). Empty hints show Claude's own reading; repeats with the same photo and words come from the server's cache and cost nothing.
- Task 1 result (10 CC0 photos, empty hints): every dot sat on its thing from the start; the questions did not. The old "second person" rule and its own example "What were you laughing at?" made Claude cast the visitor as strangers ("What were you two talking about?") or ask what a dog smelled: 4 of 10. Speaking of people as he, she or they and banning guesses brought it to 9 of 10 (the miss: a "he" under a lamp with nobody there).
- Phone check: under `(pointer: coarse)` every studio and guide field is 16 px, since iOS zooms the page into a smaller field on focus; headless at 390x844 a field's computed font-size shows it.
